## What the top-5% result means

The conclusion reports that selecting the highest-risk **5%** captures **52.6% of future failures**. In ranking terms, this is approximately **10.5× capture lift** relative to random selection of the same share:

`lift = 0.526 / 0.05 = 10.52`

This lift is an arithmetic derivation from rounded reported values, not an additional measured result. The 52.6% figure is recall/capture among all failures; it is not precision or an individual firm's predicted probability of failure.

The thesis describes the ranked units as firms. The current notebooks evaluate firm-year rows. Preserve that distinction until the original evaluation population and any firm-level aggregation are documented.

### Implementation distinctions

- **Five EBM seeds:** Table 19 uses “ensemble 5 seedov”. Notebook 03 refits EBM for a feature-stability assessment with seeds `[0, 1, 42, 123, 2026]`. Its published final prediction path uses `ebm_final`; that stability loop does not average those five models' predictions. The portfolio therefore describes a five-seed stability assessment.
- **BiLSTM SHAP:** The implementation trains an additional single-seed explanatory model. Its SHAP values should not be presented as exact explanations of the five-seed calibrated ensemble.
- **Eight EBM interactions:** This is a reported fitted-model property, not a fixed guarantee for every rerun of hyperparameter tuning.
- **T−2:** This is a reported attribution pattern, not evidence of a causal effect or a definition of the prediction horizon.

## Data and scope

The thesis uses financial statements from Slovakia's Register of Financial Statements for **2011–2019**. Failure is defined as entry into bankruptcy or restructuring in the following period. The excerpts do not specify the exact duration of that period or the operational event-date matching rule.

The conclusions are tied to Slovak enterprise data and the **2018–2019** test period. Later crisis shocks and current deployment performance were not evaluated in the supplied material. Extending the time horizon and examining crisis-period stability are stated directions for future work.
